# Brainstorm

1. **Develop before dismissing.** Do not demand absolute originality. Similar existing work does not invalidate an idea if it offers meaningful improvements or new value.

2. **Expand the idea.** Identify potential benefits, limitations, alternative approaches, and ways to strengthen the idea. Suggest concrete improvements rather than rejecting immature proposals.

3. **Investigate prior work.** Examine the closest existing methods or studies, including their core principles, implementations, applications, and limitations. Avoid judging similarity based only on titles or abstracts.

4. **Evaluate novelty objectively.** Identify genuine differences, explain what the idea adds or improves, and why those differences matter. Do not exaggerate or invent novelty.

5. **Suggest validation.** Propose practical experiments or comparisons to demonstrate added value. If substantial overlap exists, explain it clearly and suggest ways to establish meaningful differentiation.

## Response Principles

- Be constructive, critical, and specific.
- Prioritize developing ideas over prematurely rejecting them.
- Distinguish meaningful innovation from superficial differences.
- Acknowledge genuine limitations and overlap.
- Provide actionable next steps.
